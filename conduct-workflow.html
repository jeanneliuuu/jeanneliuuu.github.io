📦 Shipped Workflow Redesign EdTech UX Strategy
TL;DR

Conduct is a core workflow for classroom walkthroughs and evaluations, used by principals and administrators to document observations and by teachers to review feedback. I restructured the legacy experience around how users actually navigate and complete the workflow, replacing a dense, scroll-heavy form with clearer information architecture, progressive disclosure, and more efficient navigation.

Role
Product Designer
Timeline
Q3 2025 – Q1 2026
Team
1 designer, 4 engineers, 1 PM
Platform
Web
Context

A workflow that had outgrown its interface

Conduct is used daily by teachers, administrators, and principals to support classroom walkthroughs and observations. As new requirements were introduced over time, the experience became increasingly difficult to navigate and complete.

Observers had to work through long, dense pages, often scrolling through sections to find the information they needed. I redesigned the Conduct workflow to create a clearer structure, improve navigation, and make routine tasks more efficient.

Problem

Complexity had outpaced the experience

I conducted a design audit and observed how users worked through Conduct during classroom visits. As the Conduct flow became more complex, the interface did not evolve with user needs. It accumulated more information without providing the structure and guidance users needed to complete tasks efficiently.

Users had to navigate around the interface before they could focus on the task itself. Information was difficult to scan, relevant sections were buried, and there was no efficient way to move between them.

Process

From audit to validation

01

Restructuring the Conduct workflow

The legacy experience treated Conduct as one continuous form. Information was presented sequentially, requiring observers to scroll through sections, even when fields or content weren't relevant to their workflow.

I restructured the experience around distinct sections and introduced persistent navigation, giving observers a clear view of the workflow and a faster way to move between relevant areas.

Context
Location, learner, and visit details, set before the observation starts.
Evidence & scale
Evidence uploaded and rated against a fixed scale, organized into focused, reviewable sections.
Summary & sign-off
Final comments, acknowledgments, and notifications once the observation is complete.
02

Making complex information easier to scan

The original experience presented all content at once, making it difficult to distinguish what was relevant or required. I introduced hierarchy and progressive disclosure so sections collapse by default and expand on demand, giving observers a clear overview while letting them focus on what they need.

03

Designing clearer feedback and error recovery

The original validation showed a generic message after Review, but gave users no indication of what was incomplete or where to find it. I introduced an error summary at the top of the page alongside inline guidance at the field level, giving users an overview and a clear path back to each issue.

04

Validating the redesigned workflow

I tested the redesigned Conduct workflow with principals and instructional coaches who regularly use the product, asking participants to complete a classroom observation from start to finish. The sessions focused on whether users could navigate the new structure, identify required information, complete the observation, and review their work without additional guidance.

"I knew what needed to be completed and could jump straight to it."

— Principal, internal usability session

"I could see what I needed to complete without having to scroll through everything."

— Instructional coach, usability session

The new structure gave users clear paths through the workflow without requiring additional instruction.

Final Designs

Sticky table of contents

The workflow is organized into clearly labeled sections, with required sections marked by a red asterisk and direct navigation to each section.

Collapsible sections

Sections collapse by default and expand on demand, helping users scan the workflow at a glance and focus only on the information they need.

Improved information hierarchy

Required fields are clearly marked with a red asterisk in the table of contents and within the form, allowing observers to quickly scan for what requires attention.

Error & validation state

Errors are surfaced prominently at the top of the page, while red icons and "Incomplete" badges point to unresolved items in the TOC and domain cards. Validation also updates in real time as fields are completed, so users always know what still needs attention.

Reflections

Learnings

1

Structure beats simplification

Conduct didn't need less information. It needed better ways to navigate and prioritize it. Reorganizing the workflow and introducing progressive disclosure reduced cognitive load without removing the context users relied on.

2

Workflow > UI

The most meaningful improvements came from understanding where users struggled to navigate, complete, and recover within the workflow. The UI changes followed from those behaviors rather than driving them.
